# Tax Code Checker UK — What Does My Tax Code Mean?

> 1257L is the standard code and means a £12,570 tax-free allowance. Multiply the number by ten to get your allowance.

Reference notes for the UK tax year **2026/27**, computed from HMRC's
published rates rather than copied from another site.

## How it works

| Code | What it means |
|---|---|
| 1257L | standard £12,570 allowance |
| BR | all income at basic rate, no allowance, typical second job |
| D0 / D1 | all at higher / additional rate |
| K | negative allowance, you owe more than you are allowed |
| S / C prefix | Scottish / Welsh rates |
| M1, W1, X suffix | emergency, non-cumulative |
| M / N suffix | Marriage Allowance received / given |

A **K code** is the one worth understanding. It means a taxable benefit or an underpayment exceeds your allowance, so an amount is **added** to your taxable pay rather than subtracted. There is a 50% limit: no more than half your pay can be taken as tax.

An emergency code corrects itself automatically once HMRC issues a proper one. You do not have to claim it back.

## What this does not cover

A code that looks odd is usually correct and reflects something HMRC knows about your income. Check your Personal Tax Account before challenging it.

## Source and tax year

Figures are for the 2026/27 tax year, which runs 6 April 2026
to 5 April 2027, and were checked against
[gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027) on 28 August 2026.

Rates change every April, and often at a Budget. Check the tax year a figure
belongs to before relying on it.
